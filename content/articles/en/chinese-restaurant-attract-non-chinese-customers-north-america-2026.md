# How Chinese Restaurant Brands Can Attract Non-Chinese Customers in North America in 2026

A practical guide for Chinese restaurant and hotpot brands seeking more English-speaking and multicultural customers in Canada and the U.S.

**Direct answer:** A Chinese restaurant should not attract non-Chinese customers by making the food less Chinese. It should make the experience easier to understand. Explain the occasion, how to order, what flavours to expect and what a first-time guest should choose. Use English-first discovery content, real dining scenes and staff guidance while keeping the food and cultural identity credible.

## Why do strong Chinese restaurants remain unfamiliar?

A restaurant can be popular within Chinese communities while its English name, menu and social pages give first-time customers little context. The barrier may be uncertainty rather than dislike: guests do not know what the format is, what to order, how spicy it will be or what the meal will cost.

## What should the brand explain first?

Start with the dining occasion. Is it a group celebration, a casual lunch, a late-night meal or an interactive hotpot experience? Then explain the ordering sequence, signature items, portion guidance and flavour level. This gives new customers a safe entry point without reducing authenticity.

## How should the menu support first-time guests?

Use short descriptions that explain taste, texture and preparation. Highlight a small number of starter combinations and show how much food suits two, four or six people. Staff can recommend rather than overwhelm. A long menu becomes easier when the customer understands where to begin.

## What content reaches English-speaking customers?

Show real people eating, ordering and reacting to the experience. Short videos can explain how to build a hotpot sauce, choose a broth or share dishes. Use titles containing the dish, city and occasion so content can appear in social and search discovery.

## Should the restaurant use influencers?

Choose creators whose audience lives close enough to visit and whose content can explain an unfamiliar experience. Provide the dining context and key facts, but allow an honest reaction. Track reservations, offer redemptions, website visits and repeat content use, not only views.

## How can Chinese and mainstream marketing work together?

Keep Chinese channels for community relevance while building separate English discovery paths. Campaigns can share one brand idea, but the explanation, creators, search terms and calls to action may differ. The two audiences do not need identical content calendars.

## A practical campaign example

A hotpot brand could build a First Hotpot Night series for English-speaking couples and friend groups. One landing page explains broth, ordering and budget; Reels demonstrate the experience; local creators host small tasting sessions; and staff collect first-time questions to improve the next campaign.

## What should be measured?

Measure new-customer reservations, first-visit conversion, average spend, review themes, email or membership opt-ins and return visits. Segment results by language and campaign source. The goal is not one viral post; it is a repeatable path from curiosity to a second visit.
